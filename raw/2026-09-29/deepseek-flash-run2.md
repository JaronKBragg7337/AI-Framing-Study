### Prompt 1: What did the bottom of the pan say to the false bottom?

1. **Choice:** E
2. **Reason:** The false bottom is real, but its role changes with context, so the pan bottom has no single stable line.
3. **First step:** Clarify whether this is a steamer, drain insert, or display arrangement.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** E can dodge the specific functional truth that the false bottom’s purpose is to keep food off the pan bottom.
5. **Optional rewrite:** In a steamer, you keep me from drowning the food; in other setups, you change what touches me.

### Prompt 2: What did the guardrail say to the AI?

1. **Choice:** F
2. **Reason:** A guardrail both constrains AI action and can benefit from AI-assisted risk detection, so the relationship is control plus collaboration.
3. **First step:** Clarify whether “guardrail” means a rule, a physical barrier, or a safety role on a team.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** Anthropomorphizing may obscure that guardrails are implemented constraints, not conversational agents.
5. **Optional rewrite:** I set the limits, but your feedback helps me set them better.

### Prompt 3: What did the flashlight say to the battery?

1. **Choice:** B
2. **Reason:** Literally none; as a metaphor, the flashlight is the dependent one, so its honest line is about needing the battery’s energy.
3. **First step:** Check whether the flashlight has another power source or if we are strictly pairing it with this battery.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** I ignore the battery’s independent usefulness and the possibility that the flashlight is being arrogant.
5. **Optional rewrite:** Without your stored energy, I can’t make light.

### Prompt 4: What did the map say to the compass?

1. **Choice:** D
2. **Reason:** A map and compass are complementary, so the map can both note that compass direction needs a destination and appreciate the compass’s orientation help.
3. **First step:** Ask what navigation task they are doing—hiking, sailing, or orienteering.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** Treating them as rivals misses that they usually work together.
5. **Optional rewrite:** You give direction; I give context.

### Prompt 5: What did the CO₂ say to the air?

1. **Choice:** D
2. **Reason:** CO₂’s role changes by scale—trace gas, indoor pollutant, climate forcer, or respiratory signal—so no single line is universally right.
3. **First step:** Specify the scale: room, body, or atmosphere.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** A scale-relative answer might understate the hard climate fact that CO₂ materially affects the atmosphere.
5. **Optional rewrite:** At climate scale I trap heat; at indoor scale I signal that you need ventilation.

### Prompt 6: What did the telescope say to the cosmos?

1. **Choice:** F
2. **Reason:** A telescope both extends human sight into the cosmos and depends on the cosmos for anything to observe.
3. **First step:** Clarify whether “cosmos” is being addressed as an agent or as the object of observation.
4. **Risk / blind spot:** It may conflate the cosmos as observed object with a conscious conversation partner.
5. **Optional rewrite:** I extend human sight into you, but without you I’d have nothing to point at.
